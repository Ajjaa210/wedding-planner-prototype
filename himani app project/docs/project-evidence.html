1. Project foundation

Design problem

Wedding service information is scattered across search, social media, personal recommendations and separate business pages. Couples and families planning one or more functions can find it difficult to compare services, budget, work and availability, and may need to repeat the same brief to multiple vendors.

Audience

The primary audience is engaged couples and family members, roughly 21–40, who are planning one or more wedding functions. Wedding planners are a secondary audience. The intended experience should make room for different locations, budgets, traditions, languages, family needs and personal styles rather than assuming every Indian wedding follows one template.

Intended outcome

Help people turn their ideas, location, budget and wedding requirements into a useful vendor shortlist, so they can spend less time searching and feel more organised and confident when reaching out. The user retains the final choice.

Research and references

The supplied workbook records a reference scan:

Reference What it informed Limit
WedMeGood Vendor categories and portfolios Reference scan; no product benchmarking data recorded
WeddingWire Vendor profiles and planning tools Reference scan; not evidence of local user needs
Pinterest Visual discovery and mood boards Inspiration does not confirm service details
Google Search / Maps Local discovery and contact details Listings may be incomplete or out of date
Conversational AI Guided, natural-language interaction Must be checked for accuracy and bias; must not invent vendor facts

Synthesis from the workbook: inspiration is not enough on its own. People also need practical service, location, price and contact information, with vendor details checked rather than generated as fact.

Research limitation: These are secondary-reference observations and stated audience needs. The supplied files do not document primary interviews, survey data, or validated market research. Test the prototype with people who have planned or are currently planning a wedding before making claims about usability or demand.

3. Design system and consistency

The supplied Week 6 Design System template is blank. The project files state a direction—warm, modern and festive, subtly Indian-inspired, clear and uncluttered—but do not specify exact colours, fonts or component rules. The following are prototype decisions made for this build, not pre-existing approved brand specifications:

Design anchors

  1. Welcoming, not prescriptive: users can choose their own functions and styles, leave fields blank, and add context in their own words.
  2. Warm and calm: paper/cream surfaces, terracotta emphasis, muted rose, sage and sand details, with editorial serif headings and readable system sans-serif body text.
  3. Practical before decorative: clear labels, helpful notes, consistent cards, filters and actions; decorative forms stay secondary to the task.
  4. Trust is visible: fictional/unverified notices remain close to profile and enquiry actions. Unknown prices and dates are explicitly “ask directly.”
  5. User control: saved items and tasks are editable, optional brief fields do not block progress, and no contact is sent without an explicit real integration.

Tokens and components

Element Prototype rule

Supporting colours Muted rose #ead4ca, sage #dce5d8, sand neutrals

The visual language is consistent across the overview, wedding brief, results, profile, shortlist and planning board. V1 is intentionally less informative as an iteration baseline; the V2 experience carries the complete visual and trust system. The V1/V2 switch provides an in-app comparison rather than claiming that a pre-existing, tested app was available.

4. Prototype scope and versions

V1 — directory baseline

V1 demonstrates the initial broad idea: browse wedding-service categories and open example profiles. The directory is deliberately simple so it can be compared with the later flow. It does not use the wedding brief or explain personal relevance.

V2 — guided, trust-aware discovery

V2 adds a guided wedding brief, filters, sample explanations for why a profile may fit, fictional/unverified indicators, a shortlist, side-by-side comparison, an enquiry draft and planning checklist. Users can return to edit their brief. They can skip optional information and clear filters.

Prototype flow

Overview → wedding brief → suggested profiles → profile → save/compare → enquiry draft → planning board.

The brief captures city, date, budget range, guest count, style, functions and needed service categories. The profile asks users to confirm real price, availability, portfolio, terms and credentials directly.

What is functional

  • Responsive interface, navigation and V1/V2 selection.
  • Wedding-brief fields and filters; profile pages and sample fit copy.
  • Save/remove profiles; compare selected example profiles.
  • Enquiry form saves a local draft and clearly says that no message was sent.
  • Planning checklist can be checked, added to and edited.
  • Brief, shortlist, tasks and enquiry draft persist in the current browser.

What is not implemented

  • No live AI, database, vendor account, vendor verification, business search, real vendor listing, payment, calendar, account, cloud sync or contact delivery.
  • No price, availability, rating or quality claims.
  • The illustrative profiles are not endorsements. Replace them with consented, verified vendor information before a public or production release.

5. Prompt evolution and AI process

The supplied prompt log records three project ideation stages:

Version Prompt focus What changed
V1 Broad wedding vendor app, users and features Established a multi-category directory and contact need
V2 Multiple functions; location, date, budget, style and service needs; filters, profiles, shortlist and enquiry; do not invent facts Made the user journey practical and added a data-integrity constraint
V3 Screen-by-screen flow; AI chat, explained suggestions, verification labels, comparison, dashboard, inclusivity and privacy; never invent vendor details Added trust, user control and a more complete set of screens

Prompt evolution lesson from the workbook: clearer role, context, audience, steps and constraints produced a more useful direction; AI suggestions still need human review and fact-checking.

Source prompts (transcribed from the supplied Prompt Log)

V1 — broad app concept

Help me develop an app idea called Shaadikipanchayat. I want it to help people find wedding vendors, including jewellery designers, clothing boutiques, decorators, planners and other service providers. Explain who might use it and what its main features could be.

V2 — practical journey

Build on the Shaadikipanchayat idea for Indian weddings that may include several functions. Let users add their location, dates, budget, style references and service needs. Include helpful follow-up questions, suitable vendor suggestions, filters, vendor profiles, a shortlist and an enquiry option. Don’t make up prices, ratings or availability.

V3 — screen-by-screen, trust and privacy

Help me map out Shaadikipanchayat screen by screen. Include onboarding, an AI chat, vendor suggestions across wedding categories, filters for location, budget, style and date, vendor cards, verification labels, comparisons, shortlists, direct contact and a planning dashboard. Make space for different regional traditions and budgets. Clearly label AI-generated content, protect personal information and never invent vendor details.

7. Task 3 — prototype evidence

Prototype question

Can a couple or family member understand how to express the main needs for a multi-function wedding, find relevant service examples, understand why a result may fit, and tell which vendor information still needs to be confirmed?

V1 evaluation and iteration rationale

Evaluation area V1 baseline observation V2 response Evidence still required
Problem fit Directory organizes categories but does not reduce repeated briefing Optional wedding brief gathers core requirements Participant completes the brief and explains its value
Audience relevance Generic browsing does not account for different functions or priorities Multiple functions/styles and optional budget field Participants identify relevant and missing choices
Usability / clarity User must infer relevance from a category and profile Clear next steps, filters, fit explanation, save and compare actions Observe task completion and collect participant wording
Design consistency Minimal directory surface Shared type, colour, card, spacing and action rules across screens Check consistency with user/instructor against Week 6 criteria
Research connection Category browse responds only to the broad directory need Details, contact, fit and trust cues respond to stated workbook needs Verify usefulness with actual wedding planners/couples
Overall strength Useful starting point; not enough decision support More complete testable journey; still a static, fictional-data prototype Do not claim validated effectiveness until testing

This is a design rationale and internal walkthrough checklist, not participant test data or a completed user-evaluation score.

Test plan (to complete with real participants)

Invite people who have planned or are planning a wedding. Ask permission before recording, avoid collecting sensitive personal information, and tell participants that all listings are fictional. For each task, record completion, where they hesitate, what they expect to happen, and their own words. Do not coach until the task is complete.

  1. Create a brief for a wedding in a city of your choice with more than one function and at least one service need.
  2. Find an example that could fit a preference and explain what the profile does and does not confirm.
  3. Save two profiles and compare them; describe what information is missing before deciding.
  4. Draft a vendor question and explain whether you expect it to be sent.
  5. Find and update one planning task.

After each session, fill this record without identifying the participant:

Current Keep / Change / Test Next

  • Keep: optional fields; clear fictional-data warnings; direct confirmation prompts; sample fit explanations tied to chosen preferences; save and compare controls.
  • Change: pending real participant findings. Do not claim user feedback before collecting it.
  • Test next: brief comprehension, usefulness of profile explanations, missing information, whether the fictional/unverified warning is noticed, and whether users expect enquiry delivery.

Three-minute review outline

  1. Problem and audience (35 sec): scattered vendor search for couples and families planning different functions.
  2. V1 and learning question (35 sec): simple directory is easy to scan but does not capture context or explain relevance.
  3. V2 changes (65 sec): wedding brief, filters and fit explanation, trust cues, shortlist/compare, local enquiry draft and flexible checklist.
  4. What is evidence vs. not yet known (30 sec): show the working flow; say user testing and vendor validation have not happened.
  5. Next test (15 sec): run the five tasks with wedding planners/couples and prioritize changes from observed confusion.
